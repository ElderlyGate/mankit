# licenses: kind release-trial with days

## Why
Every release of an app carries its own signed trial key and starts a 14-day trial by itself (ADR-L9). The `licenses` package must know the new kind the contracts schema gives it, so it reads, checks and shows such a key the same way in every app.

## Changes
- `Kind`: a new value `release-trial`, and `License.Days` as a signed field (the trial length, 14 by default; only a release-trial has it).
- `License.Check()`: a release-trial names exactly one product and no feature ids, has `days` of at least 1, and no other kind has `days`.
- `Days()` and the state: for a release-trial the signed `until` is the last day it may start a trial; the running trial's first day is the file's started day and its last day is that day plus `days` less one, so `DaysLeft`, `Ended` and `Early` count from the started day, not from `issued`.
- A release-trial opens every enterprise feature of its one product only, never another app's (ADR-L2); a key for one app's release is refused by another app.
- `license show` lists it with its kind, its release (`for`) and its days left.
- `Sign` and `Verify` carry `days` as they carry every payload field.

## Waits on
The contracts card for the release-trial kind (License payload: kind release-trial with days).

- [ ] The package checks, verifies and shows a release-trial, with tests for each refusal and for the days counted from the started day, run with a temp HOME; a v4 minor; CI green.
